# Business Case — ClaimFlow

**Business Case ID:** BC-AINE-005
**Domain:** Insurance — Claims Operations
**Full requirements:** see `specs/brd/brd.md` (this document summarizes problem and value
proposition per capstone deliverable 8.1.2; it does not restate scope, alternatives, or edge cases).

## Problem

Horizon Insurance has no single pipeline that takes a claim from first notice of loss (FNOL) to
payout across its three product lines — motor, health, and life. Each product line has different
document requirements and payout math, and today there is no guarantee that:

- A claim is only paid out against a policy that was active on the incident date.
- The right supporting documents were verified before a claim is assessed.
- Fraud screening is applied consistently and explainably, rather than ad hoc or not at all.
- The payable amount is computed correctly and can never go negative.
- A decision (approve / send to review / reject) comes with a reason code an auditor or customer
  can inspect.
- Settlement records and the full history of a claim (fraud score, assessment, decision, payout)
  are immutable and auditable.
- A disputed, already-settled claim can be reopened without destroying the original record.

Without this, claims processing stays manual and inconsistent, decisions are not explainable, fraud
exposure is uncontrolled, and there is no audit trail for internal review or regulators.

## Value Proposition

ClaimFlow gives Horizon Insurance one FNOL-to-settlement pipeline, shared across motor, health, and
life claims, that:

- **Enforces policy validity** before any claim proceeds (`PolicyNotActiveException` on a lapsed
  policy at the incident date).
- **Standardizes document verification** per claim type (motor: police FIR + invoice; health:
  hospital bill + discharge summary; life: death certificate).
- **Screens every claim for fraud** with a deterministic, inspectable rule set — not a black box —
  flagging anything over a configured threshold.
- **Computes payouts correctly and consistently**: `payable_amount = min(claim_amount,
  sum_insured) − deductibles − co_pay`, with a hard invariant that it never goes negative, computed
  in fixed-point decimal (never floating point).
- **Makes every decision explainable**: `AUTO_APPROVE` / `MANUAL_REVIEW` / `REJECT`, each with a
  reason code.
- **Keeps an immutable audit trail**: claim records, fraud scores, assessments, and settlements are
  append-only; nothing is overwritten or deleted.
- **Supports disputes without data loss**: reopening a settled claim creates a linked sub-claim
  rather than mutating the original.
- **Gives admins a controlled override path**: any decision can be overridden, but only with a
  command and a recorded reason code.

## Target Users

| Role | Who they are | What they do in ClaimFlow |
|------|--------------|----------------------------|
| Customer / Claimant | Policyholder | Files an FNOL, tracks status/decision/reason codes/outstanding documents, disputes a settled claim |
| Assessor | Internal claims handler | Reviews a claim plus its fraud flags and assessed amount, submits a decision |
| Admin | Internal supervisor | Monitors claim/fraud/payout queues across products, overrides decisions with a reason code |

## Success Criteria

ClaimFlow is a capstone deliverable (BC-AINE-005) evaluated by automated rubric review rather than
live usage, so success is measured as:

- All 10 acceptance criteria (AC-01..AC-10) working end-to-end for all three claim types.
- All 8 non-functional requirements (NFR-01..NFR-08) satisfied and independently verifiable.
- A complete, working local deployment (`init.sh`) with zero hand-written production code — every
  line generated by Claude Code agents under human supervision, evidenced by specs, sprint
  contracts, and PR-driven merges.
